Branding is the overall impression people form when they encounter your business. A logo is one part of it, but the experience also includes your colours, language, photographs, packaging, service and the way your business communicates.

## Start with a clear position

Write down who you serve, what problem you solve and why someone should choose you. Keep the answer specific. A neighbourhood bakery, a premium interior studio and a repair service should not communicate in the same way.

## Build a small, usable identity system

A practical starter system includes:

- A primary logo and a simplified version for small spaces.
- A restrained colour palette with accessible contrast.
- One or two readable typefaces.
- Consistent spacing, image treatment and layout rules.
- A short guide showing correct and incorrect logo use.

## Make the identity work everywhere

Test the design on a storefront sign, visiting card, invoice, social profile, website header and packaging label. A visual identity should remain recognisable at both small and large sizes.

## Consistency matters more than constant redesign

Use the same core visual rules over time. Update the identity when the business has genuinely changed, not simply because a new trend has appeared.

## A useful first step

Collect the materials customers already see: signage, cards, menus, product labels, website pages and social posts. Look for inconsistent colours, names, contact details and tone. Fix those gaps before adding more design elements.

A strong brand makes it easier for customers to recognise you, remember you and understand what to expect.
